On timing: the Leader Letter says Level 1 was reviewed "in April" in 2025 and 2026, and "in May" in 2023 and 2024 (https://www.toastmasters.org/membership/leadership/The-Leader-Letter/Leader-Letter-June-2026/Club-News-June-2026 ; https://toastmasters.org/leadership-central/the-leader-letter/leader-letter-june-2024/club-news-june-2024). The judges only see Level 2 candidates' basic information and area of expertise. You cannot update your application between Level 1 and Level 2 in the same year (Handbook p.6).

## 3. Scoring

**Pass rule:** each judge scores out of 100. You pass a level with **80 or more from 4 of 5 judges** (Handbook pp.5–6). Candidates are not ranked against each other, and there is no cap on how many pass (Handbook p.7).

| Category | Level 1 points | Level 2 points | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Content (45)**: Personal/Audience Response | 20 | 15 | L1 & L2 ballots |
| Content: Speech Development (structure, organization, support) | 10 | 10 | ballots |
| Content: Speech Value (ideas, logic, originality) | 15 | 15 | ballots |
| Content: **Subject Matter Expertise** (Level 2 only) | n/a | 5 | L2 ballot |
| **Delivery (35)**: Physical (body language, appearance) | 10 | 10 | ballots |
| Delivery: Voice (flexibility, volume, rate, inflection) | 15 | 15 | ballots |
| Delivery: Platform Style (directness, assurance, enthusiasm, stage presence, visual aids) | 10 | 10 | ballots |
| **Language (20)**: Appropriateness | 10 | 10 | ballots |
| Language: Correctness (grammar, pronunciation, word choice) | 10 | 10 | ballots |

Other notes on judging:
- At Level 1, judges review the **whole application**: recommendations, the engagement list, the video, slides, and website. "Judges may take your entire application into consideration when finalizing your score" (Handbook pp.4–5).
- The Level 1 ballot has a "General Considerations" check: was the speech "worthy of being evaluated by a live panel of judges at Level 2?" Judges are told to recheck their scores if this answer disagrees with their total (L1 ballot p.3).
- The Level 1 ballot says equipment quality "should not be counted against candidates" (L1 ballot p.1). The Handbook, however, lists "poor audio or video quality" as a *disqualifier* at the eligibility check (Handbook p.2). So a recording must clear a minimum quality bar to be accepted, and above that bar judges should not mark it down.
- At Level 2, the Subject Matter Expertise item refers to "the area of expertise identified by the candidate **in their introduction**," so the introduction needs to state your expertise (L2 ballot p.1).

## 4. What counts and what doesn't

**Counts** (Handbook pp.1–2):
- Educational presentations, training sessions, corporate lectures, motivational speeches, and similar work ("not limited to").
- Keynotes, workshops, and seminars are the formats the application asks about (Application).
- **Virtual or online engagements count** if 20 or more people watched live. The Handbook defines "live" to include "both on-premise and virtual events in which the audience is present in real time" (Handbook p.1). Recordings watched later do not count.
- Work done while self-employed, including for clients of your own firm (Handbook p.2).
- A small-audience or low-fee engagement can still help if it is notable for its setting, client, or attendees. Note that on the application (Handbook p.4).

**Does not count:**
- **Toastmasters audiences.** Engagements must be "outside of Toastmasters" to "non-Toastmasters audiences" (Handbook p.1; Application). The video must also be before a non-Toastmasters audience.
- Speeches given as part of your **regular employment**, unless you are self-employed. These count neither as engagements nor as paid speeches (Handbook p.2).
- Talks under 20 minutes or to fewer than 20 people (Handbook pp.1–2).
- Presentations mostly built on someone else's content, such as a licensed or franchised curriculum (Handbook p.2).
- Anything not delivered in real time (Handbook p.1).
- Among the 15 paid engagements, more than 7 that were only reimbursement, per diem, or gift or donation (Handbook p.2).

**Grey areas (not addressed in any official source I found):**
- **Church sermons.** Nothing mentions them explicitly. [inference] A pastor's sermons at their own church would likely fall under "regular employment." A paid guest-speaker sermon to 20+ people for 20+ minutes on your own content looks eligible under the plain rules. Confirm with accreditedspeaker@toastmasters.org.
- Pro-bono, community, and nonprofit talks can count toward the 10 unpaid engagements if they meet the audience, length, and authorship rules [inference from Handbook p.1].
- A paid talk to a company where you are a contractor (not an employee) appears to count [inference].

## 5. Records to keep for each engagement

The **Rev. 10/2022 engagement log** asks for: **Date | Name of Company/Organization | Name of Contact/Client | Length of Presentation | Fee Received | Audience Size** (Application Rev 10/2022, pp.4–5).

For the video presentation, the application asks for: Date, Name of Client, Length, Fee Received (if any), Audience Size, "Who was the audience," Video URL, and whether slides were used (Application).

The Recommendation Form (Item 1209) asks the *client* for: their name and role, the company or event, presentation date, type, and topic; whether it showed subject-matter expertise; audience size; whether it met expectations; overall quality from Very Low to Very High; what they enjoyed and what could improve; **"Was the speaker paid?"**; and "Would you hire again?" (Recommendation form).

Suggested record-keeping, based on the fields above. [inference] Nothing requires you to submit proof, but these records let you fill in the log accurately and answer any eligibility query within the 7-day correction window.
- Contract or booking email that shows a **pre-arranged fee**, plus the invoice and payment receipt. This separates the 8+ fee engagements from reimbursement-only ones.
- Reimbursement, per-diem, or honorarium records, labeled as such.
- Head count: registration list, sign-in sheet, or webinar attendance report showing 20+ **live** attendees.
- Agenda or program showing your slot is 20 minutes or longer, plus start and end times.
- Your slides or outline, to show authorship.
- Client contact name, title, and email. You will need five of them to email forms directly.
- For online talks: platform attendance export and concurrent-viewer count.
- Notes on why the engagement is notable (client prestige, setting).
- Recording permission, if you might use it as the video.

Stephanie Angelo (AS 2023) said the program "requires everything from thorough bookkeeping records to outstanding presentation skills" (https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2024/jan/accredited-speakers).

## 6. Pathways: structure, time, and fastest route

- **Structure:** 5 levels: 1 Mastering Fundamentals, 2 Learning Your Style, 3 Increasing Knowledge, 4 Building Skills, 5 Demonstrating Expertise. Historically each path had at least 14 projects (10 required plus 4 electives) (https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2019/aug/welcome-to-pathways-101). You finish by completing Levels 1–5, each approved by the Base Camp manager, then the "Reflect on Your Path" project and a Path Completion request (https://www.toastmasters.org/education/education-programs-faq).
- **Paths currently for sale:** Dynamic Leadership, Engaging Humor, Motivational Strategies, Persuasive Influence, **Presentation Mastery**, and Visionary Communication. Five retired paths (Effective Coaching, Innovative Planning, Leadership Development, Strategic Relationships, Team Collaboration) can still be finished if you already bought them. Source: Education Programs FAQ above.
- **Price:** **$35 per path from July 1, 2026**, up from $20 (https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2026/may/pathways-additions-on-their-way).
- **Change since Oct 2025 that makes paths longer.** Every level of the six current English paths now requires meeting roles. Levels 3, 4, and 5 also require 10–15 minute Education Series presentations: one Successful Club Series talk each in L3, L4, and L5; one Better Speaker Series talk in L4; and one Leadership Excellence Series talk in L5. Level 5 roles include General Evaluator ×2, Toastmaster ×2, and Evaluator ×2. Roles must be done *while working in that level*. Legacy and translated paths are exempt. Sources: https://content.toastmasters.org/image/upload/club-officer-guide-to-2025-pathways-enhancements.pdf ; https://www.toastmasters.org/education/pathways/pathways-updates
- **Pace limits:**
  - Credit for only **one speech per meeting** (Education FAQ Q10).
  - Level completions must be submitted in order, although projects can be done in any order (Education FAQ).
  - Speeches **outside the club, including professional engagements, can earn project credit with prior VPE approval** (Education FAQ, "Speeches and Evaluations" Q1).
  - Online clubs "operate exactly like a traditional club" and "follow the same education program" (https://www.toastmasters.org/footer/faq/Online%20Clubs).
  - You can belong to several clubs, choosing which club gets credit for each completion (Education FAQ).
- **Online club membership counts.** Neither the Handbook nor the Online Clubs FAQ distinguishes online clubs. The AS rule is simply "member of a club in good standing" (Handbook p.1; Online Clubs FAQ). One 2023 AS, Dale Rees-Bevan, belonged to Keystone Speakers, "a hybrid club based in Winnipeg … that offers mentorship to members interested in pursuing the accreditation" (https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2024/jan/accredited-speakers).
- **Fastest path:** **Presentation Mastery** looks like the best fit and likely the quickest [inference]. Its projects are almost all speeches, with no large leadership projects such as High Performance Leadership required; HPL is only one of several Level 5 elective options. Level 5 includes **"Prepare to Speak Professionally,"** which lines up with AS preparation. Path content: https://www.toastmasters.org/pathways-overview/pathways-presentation-mastery-path. Leadership-heavy paths (Dynamic Leadership, Visionary Communication) include longer projects.
- **Vintage paths (April 2026):** "Basic Training for Toastmasters" (credential BT) and "The Communication Series: Entertaining Speaker + Storytelling" (CES). Each has 5 levels, costs $60, and currently requires Level 1 of another path first; that restriction will be removed later this year. Sources: https://www.toastmasters.org/education/education-programs-faq ; https://www.toastmasters.org/education/pathways/pathways-updates. **It is unconfirmed whether a vintage path counts as "all levels in a single path in … Pathways"** for AS. Ask WHQ before relying on one.
- **Typical duration:** Toastmasters publishes no official figure, and I found no reliable published average. [estimate] Presentation Mastery has about 15 projects, about 20 required roles since Oct 2025, and 5 Education Series talks, with one speech credit per meeting. In one weekly club that works out to roughly **12–24 months**. By joining 2–3 clubs (online clubs make this easy) and getting VPE pre-approval to count outside professional talks as project speeches, an aggressive member could plausibly finish in about **6–9 months**. The meeting roles, which must be done within each level, are now the likely bottleneck.

## 7. Why applicants fail

**Official disqualifiers at the eligibility check (Handbook pp.2, 4):**
- Edited video: added intro music or slides, multiple cameras, cuts, or audio or lighting enhancement.
- Missing the introduction.
- Incomplete talk, or a video stopped early.
- Poor audio or video.
- Engagements that are Toastmasters events, part of your employment, under 20 minutes, under 20 people, or not live.
- Fewer than 8 pre-arranged-fee engagements.
- Recommendations from unpaid clients, or submitted by the applicant.
- Missing the Jan 31 deadline. There are no extensions, and the $100 fee is not refunded.

**Reasons reported by Accredited Speakers:**
- **Poor recording quality.** Freddi Dogterom failed in 2010 "because the quality of her recording was poor." She failed again in 2014 because she "misunderstood the judging criteria" and passed in 2017 after mentoring. Joe Grondin and Donny Crandell both called a professional-quality video the hardest part. Source: https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2018/jan2018/accredited-speakers
- **Not enough real paid work yet.** Crandell: "Make sure you have the paid engagements before even filling out the application." Angela Louie found getting engagements the hardest part and failed in 2011 before passing. Same 2018 source.
- **Not enough audience value.** Robert Cravalho passed on his 3rd try. His main lesson was making the message valuable to the audience: "What do they want to hear? What can I teach them?" Same 2018 source.
- **Applying too early.** The Handbook says the program "is not designed to recognize … those who are working toward becoming professional-level speakers" (Handbook p.1). Verity Price advises applying "only … when your career is well established" (https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2024/jan/accredited-speakers).
- **Level 2 performance.** Lauren Parsons failed Level 2 in 2021, when it was online during COVID, and passed in 2024. Passing requires "outstanding" ratings across criteria (https://laurenparsonswellbeing.com/item/the-journey-to-becoming-an-accredited-speaker/). Thomas Iland failed his first application in 2018 and passed in 2019 using the judges' feedback (https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2020/jan/meet-the-six-new-accredited-speakers).

## 8. Prep advice from Accredited Speakers and districts

- **Get a mentor.** Toastmasters lists **24 AS mentors** (https://www.toastmasters.org/membership/accredited-speaker/accredited%20speaker%20mentors).
  - Michael Bayer: "Read the rules, follow them to a letter, and find an Accredited Speaker Program mentor to review everything you submit" (https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2020/jan/meet-the-six-new-accredited-speakers).
  - Sheryl Roush: "contact some current Accredited Speakers for guidance" (2018 article).
  - Mentoring by an AS must be free, although an AS may charge for separate professional services (Handbook p.11).
- **Learn the ballot.** Dogterom: "Know the program requirements and judging criteria. Develop your support team" (2018 article). Audience Response is the largest single item at 20 points in Level 1.
- **Use your best engagements and flag notable ones** in the log. Keep recommendations positive and current, and refresh old ones even though they stay on file for 5 years (Handbook p.4).
- **Check your website and social media**, because judges review them (Handbook pp.4, 10).
- **Plan the video.** Arrange a professional-level, single-camera, unedited recording of a real client engagement with an introducer. Grondin also had to condense a 2-hour workshop to fit (2018 article).
- **For Level 2**, build a strong 15–18 minute talk. Your introduction must state your area of expertise, since that item is scored (L2 ballot). Filter feedback and stay authentic (Clare Crowther, 2018 article; Parsons blog).
- **Build the business first.** Rees-Bevan: "Build your business, serve your community, and you'll know when the time is right to apply." Angelo: "be sure that you're doing it for the right reasons" (2024 article). LaCroix: "Don't do it for the money; do it for the person you become" (2018 article).
- **Cravalho's route to paid work:** he spoke at a District conference, asked attendees to bring him into their companies, and "free speeches led to paid ones" (2018 article).
- **District pages** (District 44 / Georgia, Houston, Superior Speakers) mostly repeat the official material. D44 notes that "Every candidate can pass" (https://www.d44toastmasters.org/accredited-speaker-program/). **Houston** (https://toastmastershouston.com/accredited-speaker-program/) and **Superior Speakers** (https://superiorspeakers.org/education/) still give a **Feb 1** deadline, which is outdated. The official deadline is Jan 31, 5 p.m. MT.

## 9. Suggested preparation timeline

[estimate] This is built from the rules above, not from an official schedule. It assumes you target the Jan 2028 cycle, with Level 2 in Aug 2028. If you already have about 25 qualifying engagements from Feb 2024 on, you could target Jan 2027 instead, with Level 2 in Paris, Aug 18–21, 2027.

| Horizon | Actions |
|---|---|
| Now to month 3 | Read the Handbook and both ballots. Contact 1–2 AS mentors. Start a compliant engagement log with contracts, head counts, and duration. Enroll in Presentation Mastery (or confirm ATM-B/ACB) and get VPE pre-approval for outside speeches. Consider joining a second or online club to speed up roles and speeches. |
| Months 3–18 | Build toward 25 or more engagements, 15+ paid and 8+ with a pre-arranged fee, keeping a buffer of 30+ so weak ones can be dropped. Collect client contacts. Ask five delighted paying clients to email recommendation forms soon after each gig; forms stay on file 5 years. Upgrade your website. |
| Months 6–15 | Record 2–3 candidate videos at real client events: single camera, clean audio, introducer included, 20–60 minutes, no edits. Have mentors score them against the Level 1 ballot. |

## 10. Historical and older information (kept separate; may be outdated)

- The program began in **1981**, when Ferdinand Chen and Hubert Dobson were the first designees (official list).
- As of 2017, 75 members had earned the AS. 2017 had 6 designees, then "the highest number ever in one year" (https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2018/jan2018/accredited-speakers).
- The count stood at 87 through 2019 (https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2020/jan/meet-the-six-new-accredited-speakers). Kevin Snyder was the 90th in 2022 (https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2024/jan/accredited-speakers, related-article blurb). 93 through 2023 (2024 article).
- Older rules: the application window was **Jan 1 – Feb 1**, and Level 2 was described as a "live 20-minute talk" (2018 article). Both have changed: the deadline is now Jan 31 at 5 p.m. MT, and Level 2 is now 15–18 minutes.
- The 2021 Level 2 was held **online** because of COVID (Parsons blog). The 2022 magazine notes that COVID pushed the program to adapt (https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2022/jan/accredited-speaker-program). The current Handbook requires Level 2 in person.
- Over time the program has shifted to require **paid** professional speakers, and it now gives more feedback to unsuccessful candidates (Dilip Abayasekara, 2018 article).
- A Rev. 01/2021 application is still hosted at the azureedge URL. Use Rev. 10/2022 instead.
- The name "Dale Anne Clark" on the list (Sydney, 2023) appears to be the same person as Dale Rees-Bevan in the 2024 magazine [inference].
